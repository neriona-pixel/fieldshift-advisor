8. Data glossary

NASA POWER

NASA POWER provides meteorological and solar data useful for agricultural and environmental applications.

PRECTOTCORR

Corrected precipitation estimate, expressed in millimeters per day in the daily dataset.

T2M_MAX and T2M_MIN

Daily maximum and minimum air temperature at 2 meters, expressed in degrees Celsius.

Frost-free season

Days between the last spring frost and the first autumn frost (T2M_MIN below 0°C). Warm-season crops need a minimum length (for example about 120 days for sorghum), so crops that do not fit are excluded from the generated rotations.

How rotations are generated

Year 1 is your current crop. The tool lists every four-year sequence that can be built from the crops fitting the local frost-free season, avoids repeating a crop family back to back where possible, scores each one for three objectives (low water demand, soil health, and income with forage), keeps the best one per objective, and makes sure the three differ from each other. Your priorities then rank them against the baseline.

GWETROOT

Relative root-zone soil wetness indicator. It should not be interpreted as a direct percentage of plant-available water.

EVLAND

Evaporation from land surfaces. Its interpretation and units depend on the selected temporal product.

Confidence and uncertainty

Confidence labels in this prototype are indicative and based on variation in the available annual climate indicators. They are not statistical validation of crop performance.

Decision-support prototype — not an agronomic prescription. NASA POWER climate data are useful for understanding regional conditions, but they do not replace field soil tests, irrigation records, local agronomic expertise or crop-specific economic analysis. Crop ratings, frost-free requirements and rotation scores are indicative and require local validation. NASA POWER data have a regional spatial resolution and may not represent conditions in an individual field.
